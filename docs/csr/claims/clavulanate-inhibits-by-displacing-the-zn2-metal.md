# Clavulanate inhibits CSR-1 by displacing the metal from the Zn2 site, and Pedroso et al. attribute that to the lysine

Pedroso et al. (bioRxiv 737403, 2019), verbatim: *"The inhibitory effect, based
on the constant Ki, is 350 uM for CSR-1trunc, 201 uM for SPR-1trunc and 290 uM
for SER-1trunc"*, with the mechanism given as displacing the Zn(II) ion in the
beta site, which the paper attributes to the residue at 263 [226] - the lysine.

Independent of any crystal geometry, this is functional evidence that the lysine
is **in** the Zn2 site and competing for it: a residue elsewhere could not make
that site the one a clavulanate displaces a metal from. It is the only
non-structural support the occupancy claim has.
