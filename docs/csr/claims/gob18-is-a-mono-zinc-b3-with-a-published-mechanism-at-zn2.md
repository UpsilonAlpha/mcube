# GOB-18 is a mono-zinc B3 with a published mechanism, and its single metal sits at Zn2

**So "a heretofore unreported B3 monometallic mechanism" does not hold as
stated.** Lisa et al., Nat Commun 2017 (PMC5599593), verbatim:

> This enzyme can be active either as a bi-Zn(II) or as a mononuclear enzyme
> with the metal ion located in the Zn2 (DHH) site

and on the Zn1 site of that subclass:

> Subclass B3 GOB enzymes have a Gln residue at position 116, replacing the
> usual His ligand

The paper proposes a mechanism covering mononuclear MBLs, so a monometallic B3
mechanism exists in the literature. EXAFS of resting mono-Zn GOB-18 gives
"4 N/O at a mean distance of 2.01 Å (including 2 His ligands)", consistent
with the Zn2 crystallographic sphere.

**Why this does not sink the novelty, and what it changes:** GOB-18 is
monometallic at **Zn2 (DHH)** with an unusual Zn1 (Gln116), while CSR-1 is
monometallic at **M1** with **two histidines and an arginine**. So the claim has
to be about the site and its ligand set, not about the metal count. See
[[the-novelty-is-a-monometallic-m1-site-with-two-histidines-and-an-arginine]].

**One check outstanding.** The 2010 JBC GOB paper and the 2016 ASM GOB structure
paper both returned HTTP 403 and were not read. A secondary source placed GOB's
single zinc at **Zn1** instead, so the site assignment currently rests on Lisa
2017 alone and must be confirmed against the primary GOB papers before this is
written up.
