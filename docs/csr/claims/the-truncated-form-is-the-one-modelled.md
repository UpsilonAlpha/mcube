# The truncated construct is the one being modelled, and truncation bears on expression rather than on catalysis

Replaces [[the-modelled-non-truncated-form-is-catalytically-relevant]], which was
the thesis's one unsupported premise.

**The user's decision, 2026-11-06:** truncation does not matter for catalysis and
matters for expression; use the truncated sequence for consistency.

**What is actually in the literature**, so the premise is not resting on the
decision alone. For the B3-RQK sibling **SPR-1**, activity was only observed for
the construct truncated by 49 residues at the N-terminus - which is a statement
that truncation affects *measurable* activity, and is as consistent with
expression and solubility as with catalysis. For CSR-1 itself, the paper reports
**no metal ions in the crystal structures of either CSR-1 or CSR-1_trunc**, so
the two constructs are not distinguished by anything at the site.

**Why `open` rather than `supported`.** No source read so far says truncation is
catalytically neutral; what exists is an absence of difference at the site and a
sibling enzyme where the truncated form is the one that works. The real
requirement is weaker than the heading and is met: **the construct modelled must
be the construct the withheld kinetics were measured on.** If those kinetics are
on the truncated protein, this premise is satisfied by consistency and the
catalytic question never arises.

**Open item:** confirm which construct the withheld kinetics belong to. This
needs no kinetic values, only the construct name.
