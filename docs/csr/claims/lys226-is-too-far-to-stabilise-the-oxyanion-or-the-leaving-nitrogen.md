# Lys226's ammonium is 4.7-5.6 angstroms from the carbonyl and the leaving nitrogen, so it cannot do Zn2's catalytic job by contact

Measured in `aspxtb8`:

    Lys226 NZ - Asp92 OD2   2.749     <- the salt bridge, intact
    Lys226 NZ - O2x         4.650     <- carbonyl oxygen / future oxyanion
    Lys226 NZ - N1x         5.594     <- the leaving nitrogen
    Lys226 NZ - O01         5.177
    Lys226 NZ - Zn          5.044

In canonical di-zinc B3 it is **Zn2 that stabilises the anionic leaving
nitrogen** at bonding distance
([[canonical-b3-mechanism-is-dizinc-hydroxide]]). At 5.594 Å the ammonium
cannot hydrogen-bond to N1x, and at 4.650 Å it cannot hydrogen-bond to the
developing oxyanion either.

**So the cation's role divides in two, and only one half is supported here.** The
*structural* half stands exactly as claimed - the salt bridge is 2.749 Å and it
holds Asp92 off the metal, which is
[[thesis-lys226-restrains-asp92-in-place-of-zn2]], untouched. The *catalytic*
half - that the RQK cations do electrostatically what the second metal would do
([[rqk-cations-replace-zn2-electrostatically]]) - survives only as through-space
Coulomb stabilisation, with **no contact to either reacting atom.**

**Nor is anything else positioned to protonate the leaving nitrogen:** N1x's
nearest candidates are Ser186 OG at **4.698** and the zinc at **4.544**. So
[[question-what-protonates-the-leaving-nitrogen]] is not answered by this
complex, and a mechanism from here needs either a water not in the model or a
substrate rotation that brings N1x toward one of those two.
