# At identical metal coordinates, the wild-type His-Zn-His angle is 117° against the double mutant's 105°

The same pasted zinc pair sits at byte-identical coordinates in all three of our
crystals ([[our-wt-crystal-zincs-are-grafted-from-the-double-mutant]]), so the
angle it subtends is a property of the **protein** alone:

    CSR_truncated_2metal.pdb            WT CSR-1        117.2°
    dmCSR_2metal.pdb                    dmCSR-1 plain   105.4°
    dmCSR_mutant_2metal_refine_16.pdb   dmCSR-1 S214D   102.2°

A controlled comparison by construction - the metal cannot be the variable.

**Two readings, and this observation does not choose between them.** Either the
wild-type two-histidine site is genuinely more open than the double mutant's,
which would be a structural difference the mechanism has to accommodate; or the
wild-type site is **apo-shaped**, having crystallised without a metal, and 117°
is what an unoccupied site looks like. 6DN4's apo value is 120.8°, which favours
the second reading and makes this the one quantity the graft cannot certify.

Either way it is the opposite sign to our models'
([[our-his-zn-his-angle-is-13-19-degrees-too-closed]]), which are too closed.
