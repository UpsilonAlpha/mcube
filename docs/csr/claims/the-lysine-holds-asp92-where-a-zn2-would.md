# Asp92 sits the same distance from Zn1 whether a Zn2 holds it or Lys226 does - 4.1-4.6 A at OD1 in four crystals of three constructs

| structure | what holds Asp92 | Zn1-CG | Zn1-OD1 | Zn1-Zn2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ours2` dmCSR-1 S214D, 1.47 A | Zn2 at 2.06 | 4.72 | 4.12 | 3.23 |
| 6NC5 dm (Q121H/K254H), 2.07 A | Zn2 at 1.95 | 4.66 | 4.22 | 3.43 |
| 6DR8 tm, 1.48 A | Zn2 at 2.22, occ 0.45 | 5.48 | 4.61 | 4.46 |
| **6DQH WT lysine, 1.10 A** | **Lys226 at 2.79** | **5.15** | **4.28** | — |

**This is the thesis's sharpest form.** "Lys226 restrains the bridging
aspartate" understates what the crystals show: the lysine holds the carboxylate
**in the position a Zn2 holds it**, to within 0.2 A at OD1, across three
different constructs and four independent crystals. The substitution is
positional, not merely steric or electrostatic - whatever the lysine is doing, the
carboxylate ends up where the canonical mechanism puts it.

**What this does and does not help.** It makes the two-arm comparison valid, since
both arms restrain Asp92 at effectively the same Zn1 distance by different means,
and it supersedes the "moves in one shell when Zn2 is absent" reading that stood
until ¶216. It says nothing about activity: the construct in which a real Zn2
holds the carboxylate is **much more active**
([[restoring-the-zn2-site-drastically-improves-catalysis]]), so an identical
carboxylate position with and without the second metal is a point against the
carboxylate's position being what limits turnover.
