# The only published mono-zinc MBL mechanism says the nucleophile is NOT activated by the metal

Lisa et al., Nat Commun 2017, verbatim:

> We attribute this difference to the involvement of a metal-activated
> nucleophile in binuclear enzymes ... thereby suggesting that nucleophile
> activation in mono-Zn(II) enzymes does not involve the metal site

and on what the nucleophile is:

> has been proposed to be the Zn1-bound hydroxide in bi-Zn(II) enzymes ... but
> has not been identified in mononuclear variants

**This project's hypothesis is a metal-bound hydroxide at M1 in a monometallic
enzyme** ([[nucleophile-is-zn-bound-water-with-neutral-lys226]]) - precisely
what that sentence says does not happen in mono-zinc MBLs.

**That is better for the paper than novelty by absence.** A gap in the
literature can be closed by a reviewer remembering a paper; a named,
quotable disagreement is a result either way. If our barrier is accessible from
a metal-bound hydroxide at a two-histidine-plus-arginine M1 site, it is a
counterexample to a published generalisation. If it is not, the published
mechanism predicted it and the alternative routes
([[cpha-route-is-closed]], [[question-does-the-substituent-on-metal-route-survive]])
become the live ones.

The same paper also reports that in mono-Zn GOB-18 carbapenem binding proceeds
by **expansion of the Zn2 coordination sphere**, which is a different structural
move from displacing a metal-bound water, and bears on
[[the-michaelis-complex-is-four-coordinate-at-the-zinc]].
