# The RQK cations supply electrostatically what the absent Zn2 would have provided

The project's central hypothesis (D107): Arg90 and Lys226, both monovalent
cations at metal-ligand positions, between them supply the +2 the absent Zn2
would have carried - taking over Zn2's **substrate-anchoring** role while being
unable to take over its **catalytic** roles, depressing the nucleophile's pKa
and stabilising the anionic nitrogen. That asymmetry would explain broad
spectrum binding with poor turnover, and it predicts a larger effect on ring
opening than on nucleophilic attack.

**Status is `open` and the support is thin, all of it ours.** The anchoring
contact does not hold over time - Lys226 NZ to the C3 carboxylate at 2.61 A in
`nacqm`, opening to 4.26 A at closest over the next 50 ps of the same
continuous trajectory, with no other cation taking over (D111 qualifying
D107). The two cations sit at opposite ends of the site rather than acting
jointly ([[arg90-and-lys226-act-at-opposite-ends-of-the-site]]). The contacts
that did look like a clamp - 0.08 A over 45 points - are a property of one
docked pose 5.15 A from the only experimental Michaelis complex (D150/D155
retracted by D156). And D190's charge-deletion decomposition, which is the test
that would quantify it, has never been run, because the deletions must be run at
**both** ends of a reaction coordinate that does not exist
([[no-reaction-coordinate-has-ever-been-computed]]).

**What it is not blocked on:** the method. All four of R, Q, K and Arg229 are in
MM as of D190 precisely so the set can be decomposed as one.
