# In canonical di-zinc B3 the substrate carboxylate AND the leaving nitrogen are both on Zn2

Measured on the two deposited B3 complexes that carry a substrate, at a 3.4 Å
listing radius:

    7UHI  L1 + intact moxalactam (MX0)
      Zn1  His107 ND1 2.18, His181 NE2 2.35, **O1 (lactam carbonyl) 2.53**, His105 NE2 2.66
      Zn2  Asp109 OD2 2.27, His110 NE2 2.27, His246 NE2 2.48,
           **O8 (C13 carboxylate) 2.65**, **N1 (lactam N) 2.67**

    5B15  SMB-1 + hydrolysed doripenem (DQM)
      Zn1  HOH425 1.83, His72 2.04, His74 2.05, His150 2.12
      Zn2  HOH425 2.08, Asp76 OD2 2.13, His215 2.13, His77 2.15,
           **OAE (CAR carboxylate) 2.23**, **NAN (opened N) 2.33**

**So the canonical complex is bidentate across the pair: carbonyl on Zn1,
carboxylate and leaving nitrogen together on Zn2.** The carboxylate is on a
metal in both - the question "do canonical B3 enzymes bind the substrate by the
carboxylate" is answered yes, and it is Zn2 that does it.

**Why this matters here.** Our crystal-derived pose reproduces the **Zn2** half
on the single metal - carboxylate 1.847 Å, N1 3.254 Å - and gives the Zn1 half
to Arg90 at 2.98 Å
([[arg90-is-on-the-carbonyl-and-cannot-reach-the-leaving-nitrogen]]). The two
jobs we propose for one metal are not a construction: they are measured
together on one metal, twice, in deposited structures.

It also removes the subclass argument from the CphA comparison. A single metal
holding a substrate carboxylate and its nitrogen is **L1's Zn2**, not a B2
peculiarity ([[the-cpha-conditions-are-met-in-the-crystal-derived-pose]]).
