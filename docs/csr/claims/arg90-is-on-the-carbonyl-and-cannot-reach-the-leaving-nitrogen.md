# Arg90 sits on the carbonyl oxygen, anti to the leaving nitrogen, and cannot stabilise it

Measured in the crystal-derived pose:

    Arg90 NH1 - N1  **5.282**     - O1  **2.984**
    Arg90 NE  - N1    6.518       - O1    4.272
    Arg90 NH2 - N1    7.508       - O1    5.189
    (O1...N1 inside the amide = 2.363)

**The guanidinium approaches the amide from the oxygen end, 2.3 Å past the
oxygen on the far side from the nitrogen.** No rotation of that side chain
reaches N1 while keeping the carbonyl contact, because O1 and N1 are at opposite
ends of the amide and Arg90 is already committed to one of them.

So the arginine's role is **carbonyl polarisation**, not leaving-group
stabilisation - the two jobs a second zinc does in canonical di-zinc B3 split,
and the arginine takes only the first.

**Lys226 is on the nitrogen's side but far:** NZ - N1 **5.830**, NZ - O1 7.427.
Its rotamer comes from the actuator lineage and is untested
([[lys226-is-too-far-to-stabilise-the-oxyanion-or-the-leaving-nitrogen]]), so
this is the one number that could still move - but 5.83 Å is a long way to come.

**This CONFIRMS the central hypothesis rather than denting it, and extends it.**
[[rqk-cations-replace-zn2-electrostatically]] already predicts exactly this
asymmetry - that the cations take over Zn2-s substrate-anchoring role "while being
unable to take over its catalytic roles ... stabilising the anionic nitrogen". The
measurement is that prediction met: Arg90 is 5.282 Å from N1 and cannot stabilise
it.

**What it extends:** Arg90 is not only anchoring. At **2.98 Å on the lactam
carbonyl** it is doing a catalytic job - polarising the carbonyl, which canonical B3
does by coordinating it to Zn1 - so the asymmetry is sharper than "anchoring yes,
catalysis no". It is **polarisation yes, leaving-group stabilisation no**, and the
metal covers the second
([[the-metal-can-both-carry-the-nucleophile-and-stabilise-the-leaving-nitrogen]]).
