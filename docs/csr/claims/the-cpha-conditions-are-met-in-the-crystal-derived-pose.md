# Judged on chemistry rather than on subclass labels, the CphA-like conditions are met in the crystal-derived pose

**¶281 dismissed this route on the wrong test.** It argued that CphA is a
Zn2-site enzyme and CSR-1 is a Zn1-site enzyme, so the architecture was absent.
That is a label, not a mechanism: B3 is a different family and the site index
carries no chemical content on its own. **The user-s reformulation is the right
test** - does the metal bind the substrate carboxylate, and is there an aspartate
placed to activate a water?

**Both hold**
([[the-crystal-derived-pose-puts-the-carboxylate-on-the-metal]]):

    Zn - substrate carboxylate O8   **1.847**
    Asp92 OD1 - Zn                  **4.33**
    Asp92 OD1 - lactam N1           **4.52**

A 4.3-4.5 Å gap between a carboxylate and the substrate is the right size for a
bridging water that the aspartate activates and that is positioned to attack -
which is the CphA arrangement, and also the GOB-18 one, where carbapenem binding
proceeds by **expansion of the metal-s coordination sphere** rather than by
displacing a metal-bound hydroxide
([[gob18-is-a-mono-zinc-b3-with-a-published-mechanism-at-zn2]]).

**It agrees with the only published mono-zinc MBL mechanism, which is the
strongest thing about it and also the weakest.** Lisa 2017 has the nucleophile
activated by a hydrogen-bond network rather than by the metal
([[the-published-mono-zinc-mechanism-has-no-metal-activated-nucleophile]]). If
CSR-1 runs this route, the enzyme is mechanistically conventional for a
monometallic MBL and the novelty narrows to the **ligand set** - two histidines
and an arginine, with the arginine doing the carbonyl polarisation at 2.98 Å
that a second zinc does elsewhere. That is still novel and still worth a paper,
but it is not a new mechanism.

**Open on one missing fact: no structure in this project has a water in the
Asp92 gap**, because no crystal-derived pose has been solvated
([[no-crystal-derived-pose-has-ever-been-equilibrated]]). Until one has, this is
a geometric argument about where a water could go.

**It also predicts the mutant result.** If the bridging position-s job is to
anchor the substrate carboxylate - Ser186 OG at 2.72 / 3.00 Å - then replacing
it with an aspartate puts a carboxylate beside a carboxylate, which should hurt
turnover without hurting binding. That is what the manuscript reports for
carbapenems: 3-5 orders of magnitude down in k_cat, "only ... mild changes to
binding affinity"
([[the-manuscript-reads-s214d-as-inhibition-by-the-bridging-aspartate]]).

**¶283 NARROWS THIS.** The CphA-like conditions are met, but the route itself is
no longer the best reading: the same pose has a **free zinc coordination position
putting a hydroxide 2.63 Å over the scissile carbon at 83.6° out of plane**
([[a-free-zinc-site-puts-a-hydroxide-263-angstroms-over-the-scissile-carbon]]), so
the nucleophile does not have to be a non-metal-bound water activated by Asp92.
What survives is the **metal's** role - binding through the carboxylate and
stabilising the leaving nitrogen at 3.254 Å - which is CphA-like and is now part
of a composite
([[the-metal-can-both-carry-the-nucleophile-and-stabilise-the-leaving-nitrogen]]).
Asp92 as general base is not needed by that composite and remains unsupported.
