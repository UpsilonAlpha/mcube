# Mono-zinc BcII (B1) carries its single metal at Zn1 and has a published Zn1-hydroxide mechanism

**This is the mono-zinc precedent at the right metal site that the record
previously said did not exist.**

BcII, the B1 MBL of *Bacillus cereus* 569/H/9, in its mono-zinc form binds its
single metal at the **Zn1 / 3H site** - His116, His118, His196 and a water -
and **Bounaga et al. 1998** proposed a mechanism for that species: Zn1 acts as a
Lewis acid, depresses the bound water's pKa so it is **hydroxide at neutral
pH**, and that hydroxide attacks the beta-lactam carbonyl. Mono-zinc BcII is
**catalytically active**, though full activity requires both metals, and a BcII
mutant structure preserves the short Zn1-water bond in both the mono- and the
dinuclear form - so **Zn2 is not required to supply the nucleophile.**

**What it is a precedent for, and what it is not.** BcII's Zn1 is **three
histidines**; CSR-1's is **two histidines plus an arginine**
([[csr1-zn1-site-is-h-r-h]]). So this transfers the **metal position and the
nucleophile**, not the ligand set. The surviving narrow form of the claim it
refutes is: *no published mono-zinc mechanism operates at a two-histidine Zn1
site.*

**Two caveats carried from the literature rather than hidden:** whether
mono-Zn1 MBL species are biologically relevant was still described as debated
in a 2015 review, and a 2017 study of carbapenem hydrolysis by mononuclear and
binuclear MBLs argues that in mono-zinc enzymes nucleophile activation does
**not** involve the metal site - which contradicts the Lewis-acid model, and if
correct would point back toward a CphA-like route rather than this one.
