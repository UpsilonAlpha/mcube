# A neutral lysine amine would not be found 2.8-3.0 A from a carboxylate oxygen in three independent crystals

The reasoning the protonation state rests on, stated as its own premise so it can
be attacked on its own. Lys NZ to carboxylate O is **2.99 A in 6DN4, 2.83 in
6DQ2, 2.79 in 6DQH** (the last at 1.10 A resolution). In surveys of protein
structures, N-O separations in this range between a lysine and an aspartate or
glutamate are the signature of an ion pair; a neutral amine is a weak, long
hydrogen-bond donor and sits further out, and it has no reason to pay the
desolvation cost of burying itself against an anion. Three independent crystals,
two of them apo and one metal-bound, all give the same contact.

This is the premise the user ruled sufficient, and it is the right shape of
argument: the geometry is observed, and the inference from geometry to charge is
general structural chemistry rather than anything specific to this enzyme.

**What it does not settle.** It constrains the pair, not the lysine alone - an
ion pair is equally consistent with a lysine whose pKa has been *lowered* toward
neutrality by the buried environment, which is exactly what makes a titratable
site interesting. And it says nothing about the state *during* turnover, where
the question is whether the lysine can be the proton source
([[question-what-is-lys226-pka]], [[lys226-deprotonation-precedes-the-asp92-collapse]]
if that is ever written). A citable survey number for the N-O distance
distribution still has to be found; the claim is currently supported by the
geometry plus general chemistry, not by a reference.
