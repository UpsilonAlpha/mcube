# In every CSR structure with a restored M2 site, the metal occupies M2

Measured across the deposits and the one original one-metal crystal:

    6NC5, plain double mutant, deposit
      ZN301  occ 1.00  Asp91 OD2 1.95   His92 NE2 2.01   His225 NE2 2.04   HOH493 2.08
      ZN302  occ 1.00  His87 NE2 2.22   His163 NE2 2.09  HOH493 2.14   HOH485 1.90
      Zn-Zn 3.43, bridged by HOH 493.  DI-ZINC, both sites full.
      (Four further zincs sit on surface acetate, phosphate and carboxylates.)

    dmCSR_mutant_1metal_refine_12, dmCSR-1 S214D, our original
      ZN1  occ 0.70   Asp78 OD2 2.13   His79 NE2 2.22   His212 NE2 2.12
                      + three waters at 2.07 / 2.16 / 2.38 - SIX-coordinate
      ONE zinc, and it is at M2.

    wild type
      M2 does not exist as a ligand set; only M1 is available.

**So there is no one-zinc mutant at M1**, and the reason is a result rather than
an inconvenience: **restore a canonical M2 and the metal goes there.** The one
crystal with a single metal and a restored M2 put it at M2 with three waters;
the plain double mutant fills both sites and bridges them with a water, which is
the canonical B3 arrangement.

This is the structural content of
[[csr1-is-mono-zinc-with-zn2-vacant]]: the RQK enzymes are monometallic **at
M1** because M2 is destroyed, not because the protein holds only one zinc in
some general sense. It also means a wild-type-versus-mutant comparison cannot be
matched on metal count and site at once - the metal count and the metal's site
move together.
