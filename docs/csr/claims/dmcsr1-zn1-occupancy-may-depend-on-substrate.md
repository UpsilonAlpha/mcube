# The CSR-1 manuscript reports Zn1 occupancy in dmCSR-1 S214D as substrate-dependent

From the manuscript's results (para 37): multiple dmCSR-1 S214D datasets, from
**different crystallisation conditions**, all showed **only a single zinc,
located at the Zn2 (DHH) site**. The second site was occupied in **one** dataset
only - from enzyme **co-crystallised with ampicillin**. The authors' stated
inference: "a weaker binding at the Zn1 site, and a possible mechanism where
**binding of metal at this site is facilitated by the presence of substrate**."

**Two consequences for this project.**

**It confirms our own file from the authors' text.** Our copy of the two-metal
structure has Zn2 at full occupancy and **Zn1 at 0.53**
([[our-dmcsr1-structure-puts-the-single-zinc-at-zn2]]), which is the same
result read off the coordinates.

**It is an independent parallel for substrate-coupled Zn1 geometry.** The
project has a measured defect - the His-Zn-His angle 13-19 degrees too closed
([[our-his-zn-his-angle-is-13-19-degrees-too-closed]]) - and the only crystal
with a closed angle and a ligand present is 7UHI. If Zn1 occupancy and geometry
genuinely track substrate binding, that is a reason the angle differs between
apo crystals and substrate complexes rather than being purely a force-field
artefact. **Not yet evidence about our structures**, only a literature reason
the comparison may not be apples to apples.

**One internal tension to carry, because it cuts the other way.** The text
reports **no density consistent with ampicillin** in the active site; comment
483, by the manuscript's first author, says "**There is density there** but
Stefan couldn't fit ampicillin in there." Unmodellable density is not absent
density, and it is the second reading that would support the
substrate-facilitated mechanism the paragraph proposes.
