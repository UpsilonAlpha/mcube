# Audit: CSR-1 QM/MM mechanism project

**Sources read in full:** `docs/csr-decision-log.md` (4312 lines, ¶1–¶177), `docs/csr/site.yaml` (1730 lines), `docs/running-on-bunya.md`, `docs/csr/job-inventory.md`, and the load-bearing parts of `src/mcube/{preprocess,qmmm,scan,neb,ts,sp}.py`. Branch `fix/pipeline-review` at `1ed8345`.

**Headline, before the detail.** In ~3 months and 166 Slurm allocations (55 COMPLETED, 48 CANCELLED, 15 FAILED, 11 OUT_OF_MEMORY, 2 running) **no reaction path has ever been computed for CSR-1.** `neb` has never completed and `ts` has never run — stated in the log itself ("`CSR_ligand/neb_results` holds only two force-field XML files from September and no config carries a `neb` section, so no NEB has ever completed; `ts` has never run either", entry at line 1739) and confirmed by ¶144: `step1`'s two endpoint files "do not exist anywhere on scratch". There is no barrier, no transition state, no IRC, no intermediate. Every mechanistic question in the project brief is open.

Worse, as of **today's entry ¶177**, the structure from which every QM/MM calculation in the project descends is now known to have a **six-coordinate zinc in 4995 of 5000 frames** against the crystal's four — so the existing QM/MM results describe a species that is not the one the paper is about. The project's own log says this; I agree with it, and it is the correct reading of the data.

## PART 2 — VALIDITY, RUN BY RUN

### 2.1 Metal coordination — the dominant defect, and it is systemic

**The cutoff history is a moving target and it was never a measurement.** 4.0 Å (pre-¶8, which restrained Arg90 at 3.5 Å as a "ligand") → 3.0 Å (¶8) → 2.5 Å (¶132, ¶120's prescription) → back to 3.0 Å (¶174). Each change altered which contacts became 50 kcal/mol/Å² harmonics for an entire 100 ns run. ¶129 names the governing error precisely: "Never pick a cutoff after seeing where the atoms landed."

**Per-residue detection (¶175/¶176, found 2026-11-02).** `find_coordinators` called `coordination_shell(pdb_file, inputs)` — taking the `per_residue: bool = True` default — while the function's own comment said restraints are per atom. **Only the closest donor of each residue was ever restrained.** This is the exact mechanism of both ¶120 and ¶130: Asp92's OD2 pinned at 2.80 Å while OD1, in the same residue, was free and rotated onto the metal at 1.99 Å. Both entries blamed the cutoff; ¶174 (written an hour before the discovery) blamed it again. I verified the fix in `src/mcube/preprocess.py:1184` — now `per_residue=False` — and that **no test in the suite had ever referenced `find_coordinators`, `coordination_shell` or `coordination_from_rows`.** A comment and its implementation disagreed across two decision entries about the consequence.

Who this affects, worked out independently:
- **`md_naclong` (100 ns, the parent of `nacqm`, `nacarg`, `qmmm_ohH`, ¶106's NEB start, ¶107/¶111's contacts, and both `aspout`/`aspin`):** restrained Asp92 OD2 at 2.80 Å — a contact that is **not a bond** and, per ¶145, **not even a crystal distance** — and left OD1 free. OD1 arrived at 1.99 Å; a second water (E:557) arrived at 2.27 Å. Six-coordinate.
- **`md_wt100` / `md_wt100b`:** the per-residue bug did *not* bite (four residues, one donor each, ¶176). But the 2.5 Å cutoff left Asp92 free, and **¶177 measures the consequence: CN = 6 in 4995/5000 frames, Asp92 OD2 at 100% occupancy (mean 2.07 Å), OD1 at 56.7%, a bulk water at 43.2%. Never four-coordinate.** The fix prescribed in ¶120 therefore never worked, and nothing would have revealed it: `md` reports the shell on two frames and the "before" frame is `finalsystem.pdb`, where Asp92 is still at 2.80 Å.
- **`md_prod100`:** the ligand's O5x (2.92 Å) was held and O4x (2.94 Å) left free, 0.02 Å behind it. Over 34 ns O2x travelled **4.75 Å onto the metal** while the two oxygens 5AXO puts at 2.9 Å left for 6.6 and 8.5 Å. "The complex that came out is not the complex that went in" (¶173) — correct.

**The crystal reference was itself wrong for ten weeks.** ¶145: the 2.80 Å figure every entry from ¶120 onward quotes as "the crystal's Asp92 distance" is **our own model's Asp–hydroxide hydrogen bond**. Measured in 6NC5, Zn1(ZN302)–Asp91 OD2 is **4.20 Å** and OD1 **4.22 Å**. So the model–crystal disagreement is 2.07 vs **4.20 Å**, not 2.07 vs 2.80 — and **both scan arms stop at 3.60 Å, 0.6 Å short of the crystal and still climbing at 13.8–14.0 kcal/mol.** Nothing in the project has ever put Asp92 where the crystal has it. ¶143's headline ("the crystal's 2.80 Å sits 5.4–7.9 kcal/mol up this curve") is therefore void; the real cost is unmeasured.

**Restraint laundering (¶68).** The single clearest invalidation in the record. Run 1 applied a *chosen* harmonic Zn–O2x → 2.2 Å (k=20); the carbonyl settled at 1.94 Å; run 2 took run 1's end frame as `protein_pdb`, detected 1.94 Å as "coordination", and froze it **holonomically**. Six constraints where run 1 had four. "A distance that does not move by 0.01 Å across molecular dynamics is a rigid constraint" (¶67). Everything from ¶28 through ¶70 — ¶29's Lys226 transfer, ¶30/¶35's DFT check, the H1–H4 scans, ¶48's ΔE, ¶52's mechanism, ¶57/¶58's Asp92 curve, ¶61's carbonyl scan — was measured in that state. ¶40's own diagnosis is right: "¶29/¶30 ... was computed *in* this over-coordinated state, so it does not settle the protonation question for the correct one."

**Did the zinc keep its coordination *through* the runs?** Only measurable since `site.py shell` (¶120) and `postprocess` coordination occupancy (¶167, 2026-11-01). The answer, everywhere it has been asked: **no.** ¶102 (6), ¶120 (5 in 100% of both QM/MM arms), ¶134 (5.57 mean), ¶138 (6.00), ¶173 (6 in 1704/1707), ¶177 (6 in 4995/5000). Not one trajectory in this project has held a four-coordinate zinc. Every single-frame `sphere` report that looked healthy was reading the one frame where Asp92 was still at its starting 2.80 Å.

### 2.2 Protonation and tautomers

**The nucleophile's protonation state is unresolved, and the whole `CSR_nac` chain ran in the state ¶48 rejected.** ¶48 (job 30053828) concluded "the resting state is Zn-OH2 with neutral Lys226. The model genuinely has no hydroxide", ΔE = −5.23 kcal/mol at ωB97X-D3(BJ)/def2-SVP. ¶112, **eight days and fourteen entries later**, found `CSR_nac` is in `lysH` (hydroxide + Lys226-NH3⁺) — "the verdict was never carried across". So ¶102, ¶104, ¶106's NEB start frame, ¶107, ¶110 and ¶111 are all runs of the other species.

And ¶48 itself does not settle it. Three independent reasons, all in the log:
- It was computed with **imposed sphere constraints** (Zn–Asp92 OD2 = 2.80, Zn–O2x = 2.90) on both arms, so both states were held in an arrangement the surface does not prefer.
- ¶48's own caveat: −5.2 kcal/mol is "within reach of def2-SVP basis error, the imposed constraints, and the absence of entropy, sampling and explicit environment relaxation".
- **¶107's pKa bookkeeping argues the opposite sign:** a 2+ cation 4–5 Å away should *raise* a lysine's pKa; Zn–water ~5.6 against Lys ~10.5 puts the transfer **uphill by 6–7 kcal/mol**. And GFN2-xTB's worst benchmarked functional group is exactly the protonated amine, MUE **5.3–7.7 kcal/mol** (Arantes & Rezáč 2025) — which spans the entire disagreement.

¶119 then settled only the narrow question: the two arms behave the same, so protonation is not what loses the substrate. **It did not establish which state is the reactant.** P6 is still listed as open at the end of the log and is marked "Blocks all scans".

**Asp92's charge has never been tested** (¶151, ¶154). The one run that bears on it — `qmmm_asph` (¶153) — found that a *neutral* Asp92 walks off the metal to 2.99 Å and leaves the crystal's four-coordinate sphere behind, with nothing restrained. That is a clean, interesting result and the log is right to call it a diagnostic rather than a candidate reactant: ¶154 corrects ¶153's electrostatics (a cation *lowers* a nearby carboxylate's pKa, so a metal-bound Asp is ionised). But it means the one route that produces the crystal's sphere does so via a protonation state the chemistry rules out.

**Histidine tautomers rest on hydrogens the log itself says are not experimental.** ¶23 took the tautomers from `CSR_nontruncated_1metal_original.pdb`'s refined H; ¶21 records that in that same file "every His has HE2, including the Zn-facing NE2 of His88 and His164. His164 HE2 is **0.76 Å** from the Zn, so the H atoms came from a tool's default, not from the experiment." ¶7 flags His164 as "mechanistically sensitive" — its NE2 is 3.2 Å from the carbonyl oxygen that becomes the oxyanion, and HID vs HIE decides whether it can donate an H-bond to that oxyanion — and prescribes a PROPKA/H++ run plus an HIE sensitivity check. **Neither was ever done.** This is a live referee target and it is cheap.

**The ligand's protonation and charge.** ¶13's zwitterion (COO⁻/NH₂⁺, reactant net 0, product net −1) is well reasoned and was verified independently for the product by measurement (¶159, corrected the same day — H03 is on N1x, all four carboxylate oxygens bare, net −1). Good. But the QM charge is **an unvalidated config integer**: `src/mcube/qmmm.py:966,1208` pass `inputs.get("charge")` straight through with no cross-check against the QM region's composition. The log has two near-misses on exactly this: `qmmm_asph` needed `charge: 3` rather than the inherited 2 ("a run left at 2 converges the SCF on the wrong species while looking entirely normal", ¶152), and ¶163 had to reason the product's charge out by hand. `docs/running-on-bunya.md` still states "For CSR: Zn²⁺ + OH⁻ + meropenem + Asp⁻ + His = **0**" — the charge for a QM region that has not been used since ¶12. That documentation is actively dangerous.

### 2.3 The QM region

- **Composition drifted between runs that are compared with each other:** 105 atoms (¶12) → 119 (¶23, +meropenem) → 125 (H1 scans, +2 waters) → 130 (`CSR_gln93`, hand-edited `qm_atoms`) → 137 (`nacarg`, +Arg229). The `aspout`/`aspin` hysteresis pair carries Gln93; `michaelis2d` carries Gln93; `nacqm` does not; `qmmm_nacarg` does not. ¶170 flagged the path sections (`attack`, `ringopen`, `step1/2`, `saddle1/2`) as **lacking** Gln93 while the diagnostic sections have it; ¶171 then recorded that the omission was deliberate (Gln93 is neutral and remote) and removed it from the product arm. Either way, **the sections that would produce the paper's barriers have a different QM region from the sections that produced the supporting measurements.**
- **A specific comparability flaw I found that the log does not name:** `sp_qmmm_ref`, `sp_qmmm_r229` and `sp_qmmm_q93` all score `protdft/scan_aspin/scan_00.pdb` — a geometry optimised **with Gln93 inside the QM region** — and score it **with Gln93 in MM** (the sp sections deliberately omit `extra_qm_residues` so Gln93 has charges to delete). The ΔE between ref and deleted is still meaningful because the two runs are identical; but the Gln93 number specifically (−0.87 kcal/mol) is the MM-embedding contribution of a residue that was quantum when the geometry was made.
- **The decomposition the paper's thesis requires cannot be completed.** Arg90 and Lys226 — the R and the K of RQK — are inside the QM region in **every one of the eleven systems** (`extra_qm_residues: {A: [90, 226]}`), so electrostatic embedding has already removed their MM charges and charge deletion refuses them by name (¶153). Only Arg229 (+44.34) and Gln93 (−0.87) are measurable. ¶153 states the consequence honestly: "A charge-deletion table over the RQK site cannot be completed", and mixing deletion with a parallel all-MM model in one table is not legitimate. **The central hypothesis of the paper is about R, Q and K, and the method chosen to test it can reach only the Q.**
- **Arg229 is in MM in every production run** — and is the only residue in the project with a persistent substrate contact (64.2% of 5000 frames; 3.62 ± 0.04 Å across all 45 grid points). ¶32 noted in October that it is closer to both the carboxylate and the leaving nitrogen than Lys226 "yet is not in the QM region", and ¶148 notes it still is not. "The QM region is drawn around the wrong cation" (¶148) — I agree.
- **No QM-region convergence test.** Flagged, then explicitly "accepted as a stated limitation rather than an open question" (level-of-theory entry, 2026-10-22), citing Mehmood & Kulik that too-small QM regions *over*-bind zinc. Given that over-binding zinc is the single contested observation in the project, accepting this limitation is the wrong call.
- `resolve_actatoms` did not union the QM atoms into the active region until ¶105; `actradius: 8` gives 467 active atoms against a QM region reaching 15.85 Å with 39 of 119 atoms outside the radius. It happened to be safe (whole-residue selection) but nothing checked it.

### 2.4 Level of theory

**All gradients are GFN2-xTB. DFT appears only as single points at ωB97X-D3(BJ) or ωB97X-V with def2-SVP.** The level-of-theory policy (2026-10-22) is defensible as *faithful to the source papers* (Imhof used AM1/d, Xu & Guo SCC-DFTB), and ¶47's reversal of tier B is a sound judgement on cost. But three things bear directly on conclusions being drawn:

1. **Imhof's own semi-empirical benchmark predicts exactly this model's three contested features** (¶114, read at last on 2026-10-27): AM1/d made 4→5-coordinate **7.55 kcal/mol too favourable**, made bidentate→monodentate carboxylate **7 kcal/mol too costly**, and **missed a water→Asp proton transfer that DFT finds**. Our model is five/six-coordinate with Asp92 on the metal and a contested hydroxide. This is the most serious unaddressed method risk in the project and it is the project's own template paper that raises it.
2. **The DFT checks that exist are narrower than they read.** ¶58/¶61 (Asp92: DFT 1.6× *steeper*) and ¶65 (carbonyl: agreement to 1.3 kcal/mol over a 24 kcal/mol range) are genuine and reassuring — but ¶59 and ¶64 correctly deflate them: both methods were asked the same *conditional* question ("given this octahedron, what does removing one ligand cost?"), the single points are computed **on xTB geometries** so "if the structure is in the wrong basin, DFT inherits the wrong basin", and agreement between two methods on a loaded question is not evidence the question was right. ¶59 states plainly that "the ¶58 entry overstated this".
3. **Where xTB's accuracy actually bites a conclusion:** (a) the Lys226→OH⁻ transfer (worst-benchmarked functional group, MUE spans the whole 5.2 kcal/mol signal); (b) the coordination number, where Imhof's benchmark predicts the sign and roughly the magnitude of the error observed; (c) the 25–34 kcal/mol carbonyl-binding cost of `michaelis2d`, which has **no DFT check at all** and is the basis for the strongest negative claim in the project. P1 (the cluster-model DFT-vs-xTB benchmark, Imhof-style) is still listed as not done, 177 entries in.

**One method bug worth noting because it is a near-miss on a published number:** ¶46 found that `scan`, `neb` and `ts` built the QM theory from `qmmm.inputs` **only**, so `qm_engine: dft` or any `dft_*` key set on a `scan_*`/`neb_*`/`ts_*` section was silently ignored and the stage ran at xTB. "That would have produced 'DFT' barriers that were really semi-empirical, with nothing in the output to say so." Fixed; `qm_level` is now recorded in every result. Good catch, and the right fix.

### 2.5 Sampling and convergence

- **Classical equilibration is now at literature length** (100 ns on an H100 in 3h13m, measured twice at 750–760 ns/day). That is solid and ¶101/¶102's correction of the "10–100 ns published practice" conflation (Gervasoni's 100 ns is *production*, their equilibration was ~260 ps) is honest.
- **QM/MM MD is 50–100 ps against SMB-1's 9.7 ns** (¶155). At the measured 2.57 ps/h, matching that is ~157 days, which is why SMB-1 used a 25 Å stochastic-boundary sphere. This is a real and currently unresolved gap, and ¶155's three options (truncated model / short QM/MM as a stability check with the limitation stated / measure then decide) are the right framing.
- **Optimisation convergence is weak where it matters most.** ¶106's NEB start structure: energy converged (<1e-4 Eh over three steps) but **max gradient component 1.9e-01** when the run stalled at 111/300 steps. The entry is honest — "a relaxed geometry, not a stationary point" — and then uses it as the `step1` reactant anyway. A NEB from a non-stationary endpoint, with the endpoint's energy as the barrier's zero, is not a barrier.
- **The rigid-water `$set` trap (¶137–¶142) is a model case of a real convergence invalidation.** `optimizer_constraints` emitted active-region MM waters as `$freeze` with no user constraint and switched the whole block to `$set` the moment one appeared — which a scan always supplies. A `$set` water distance becomes a *convergence criterion* checked against `convergence_cmax`; rigid TIP3P (0.9574 Å) cannot satisfy the flexible model's minimum (~0.978–1.026 Å), so **no point could converge however long it ran**: 0 of 9 points in 4h39m, 160 SVD failures, 4.6 GB of dumps. Fixing the default to `rigid_water: false` turned the identical job into 9/9 converged in 1h16m with zero SVD failures. ¶141's audit is right that the sweeping claim is false (67 earlier scan points did converge with `$set` waters), and ¶142 isolates the mechanism precisely: it bites only when the start structure came from an MM frame written with `rigidWater=True`. **The closing test was declined** ("Not needed"), so `scan_H1_f00497`, `scan_H2_f00395`, ¶81's sphere-constrained pair and ¶105's stalled endpoint remain **flagged rather than measured** — their energies are not to be trusted to any established tolerance.
- **Scan reversibility was tested once and failed in the middle.** ¶143: `aspout`/`aspin` agree at both ends (13.84 vs 13.95) and on the minimum position (2.11 Å in both) and depth (−0.86 vs −0.98), but separate by **up to 3.78 kcal/mol at 2.54–2.96 Å**, one-sided, with `aspout`'s non-monotonic step at 3.175 Å not reproduced. The conclusion drawn — single-direction scans are untrustworthy point-by-point to a few kcal/mol, which is the size of the effects the project argues about — is correct and should be applied retroactively to `scan_znasp`, `scan_znco`, `scan_zngln` and `michaelis2d`. And `michaelis2d`'s Asp92 minimum (2.75 Å) disagrees with the scan pair's (2.11 Å) by 0.6 Å on a different structure and active region, which is a further caution nobody has resolved.
- **Barriers from converged paths: none exist.** ¶17 planned "5 or more snapshots, barriers reported as mean ± spread"; ¶47 explicitly dropped "three structures per mechanism". So even when a path is computed, the current plan yields a single-snapshot barrier with no spread.

### 2.6 Controls and comparability

- **Absolute energies are not comparable between runs** because `resolve_actatoms` re-derives the active region per structure. ¶141 caught this on the hysteresis pair: `aspin` point 0 and `aspout` point 8 are the same structure at the same 3.60 Å and differ by **4.9 kcal/mol**. Each curve is readable only relative to its own first point. `site.yaml`'s own comment on `step1` flags the same hazard for the NEB endpoints and leaves it to be checked by hand.
- **The reactant and product arms are not comparable as built.** The product (`CSR_prod`) is template-anchored (5AXO transfer, ¶159), has its own OpenFF parameterisation, and after ¶174 restrains **eight** contacts at crystal distances. The reactant (`CSR_wt`) is Vina pose 2, restrains **four** at a 2.5 Å cutoff, and ¶177 shows its zinc went six-coordinate anyway. ¶177's own closing: "The reactant needs the `prod100c` treatment ... That is a 100 ns run ... and it re-bases the whole reactant arm."
- **Two different meropenem parameterisations exist** with different charges across 51 atom lines (`C1x` −0.0921 vs −0.1443), and three run directories have no parameterisation of their own and borrow another run's (¶117). `CSR_unrestrained2` used the older set. Nothing was regenerated, correctly, because which is right is a chemistry question — but it means some archived runs used a different ligand force field from others.
- **The NEB crosses a different potential surface from its endpoints.** `qmmm`/`scan`/`ts` applied optimizer water constraints; ASH's NEB takes no constraints argument at all, so interior images can lower their energy by relaxing water geometry unrelated to the reaction and a path can dip below its own reactant. `neb.check_path_shape` warns and explicitly says it cannot distinguish that from a genuine intermediate. The `rigid_water: false` default (¶139) largely removes this, but no NEB has run either way.
- **OpenFF silently renames ligand atoms** (¶168): 15 of 53 for the product, including a **permutation among names both files use** (`O01`→`O6x`, `O5x`→`O4x`, `O6x`→`O5x`). A selector written against the ligand file then names a real but different atom and nothing complains. ¶169's `ligand_atom_map` closes this. It is worth asking whether any *earlier* reactant-side selector was affected — the log says no, because the reactant's substrate comes in through a PDBQT which carries no names to map, and because `C4x`/`N1x` are unchanged. I checked the reasoning and it holds.
- **¶115's atom-index bug** is the same disease at the config level: `md_restraints` applied saved raw indices (`4203` for Zn) verbatim to a hand-edited structure in which 4203 is meropenem's C1x and 4255 is the nucleophile's *hydrogen*. The zinc was unrestrained, His164 left the metal, the run was void. The guard (¶116) then caught it on the real run. This was latent across every run that happened to reuse `processed_pdb`'s atom order.

### 2.7 The pose — the defect that subsumes most of the above

**Measured, not suspected (¶126, ¶156).** No Vina pose puts *any* atom within 5 Å of the zinc; pose 2, the one everything since ¶123 descends from, is the **furthest of the nine at 7.23 Å**. Vina was evidently run without the zinc as a feature of the site. Against the only experimental Michaelis complex in the family (L1 7UHI, moxalactam transferred into our frame, fit RMSD 0.62–0.85 Å): L1's lactam carbonyl sits at 2.27 Å from Zn1 and its C3 carboxylate at 2.82; pose 2 gives **4.90 and 8.14 Å, centroid 3.66 Å off**; and the structure actually being run (`scan_aspin/scan_00.pdb`) is **5.15 Å from the experimental pose — further than any docked pose was**.

Three consequences I would press on as a referee:
1. `michaelis2d`'s 25–34 kcal/mol for binding the carbonyl, and its "the carbonyl expels His88" finding, are measured from a ligand that is 5.15 Å from where the experiment puts it. The surface was driving the carbonyl across the site, not onto the metal.
2. ¶153's Arg229 +44.34 kcal/mol is an absolute QM–MM electrostatic interaction at **one geometry**, with the ligand in the wrong place. The log is right that it is not a catalytic contribution (only the *change* between reactant and TS enters a barrier) — but it is also not a number about this enzyme's Michaelis complex.
3. The project's own gate, `site.py check --hypothesis=carbonyl_on_metal`, has been **REJECTED in every run it has ever been applied to** (¶144). The honest summary in ¶154 is the right one: "we have an equilibrated enzyme, and a substrate that will not stay in the pose we need without being held there."

**One genuine methodological finding sits inside this.** ¶155's observation that the project applied crystallographic template transfer to the *metal* (¶23: 6NC5 superposed on 256 Cα at 0.58 Å, Zn and its two waters moved as a rigid unit) and accepted it without argument, while the *ligand* came from a docking score, is the sharpest single piece of self-criticism in the log. And ¶158's similarity measurement (CSR-1 vs L1: 39.0% identity, 221-residue core at 1.05 Å CA RMSD — **closer than L1 vs SMB-1, the cross-enzyme step the SMB-1 paper itself published**) is a solid justification for doing the transfer.

### 2.8 Things that are sound

To be fair to the record: the guards are good and several are novel. The laundering refusal (¶71), selector-not-index restraints (¶115/¶116), the NEB atom-order check, `confirm_produced` (¶154) which makes a stage that computes nothing exit non-zero, `check_ts_mode` comparing the imaginary mode against the monitored bonds, the `LIGAND_MAX = 2.5 Å` warning recorded **in the config rather than stdout** (¶131, because "¶120 sat unread in a `.out` for three weeks"), the `--hypothesis=` refusal to default (¶127 — a gate that rejected a rival hypothesis by assuming the favoured one), the `michaelis_hypotheses` labelling of `substituent_on_metal`'s range as "NOT a reference value", the minimum-image fix (¶72 — a warmup frame reported Zn–O2x as **76.74 Å**), and the compile-every-module test (¶167, after a gated test hid a syntax error). The upstream bug reports (ash#529 g-xTB silently returning gas-phase QM/MM energies; ash#530 library-mode PC gradient; xtb-python#130) are real contributions.

The project's self-correction rate is high and the corrections are mostly in the right direction. The problem is not rigour; it is that the rigour has been spent almost entirely on the pipeline and almost none of it has yet produced a result about CSR-1.

## PART 3 — WHAT IS PUBLISHABLE NOW

**Nothing mechanistic. Not a barrier, not a transition state, not a nucleophile identity, not a role for any RQK residue.** Say this plainly to the user: a paper titled "the wild-type catalytic mechanism of CSR-1 by QM/MM" cannot be written from this record, and nothing in the record is one or two runs away from changing that.

### Publishable about CSR-1 (two items, both structural, neither mechanistic)

**(a) Zn2 is vacant in wild-type CSR-1, from the deposited coordinates.** This is the strongest CSR-1 result in the project and it costs nothing. 6NC5's `SEQADV` records show HIS A92 ← GLN and HIS A225 ← LYS (our Gln93 and Lys226, the Q and K of RQK) as engineered mutations, restoring a canonical Asp-His-His Zn2 set; **the only way anyone has ever put a metal into CSR-1's Zn2 site was to engineer two of its ligands back**, while ZN302's own ligands (His87/His163) are untouched by either mutation. ZN303–306 are adventitious. This is positive structural evidence, stronger than the absence of density the project previously rested on (¶131). *Figure:* the six-zinc table from `reference-structures.md` plus the ZN301/ZN302 ligand sets. *Rests on:* a `refstruct.py shell` measurement on a fresh download, re-derivable from the PDB ID.

**(b) A measured comparison of CSR-1's Zn1 site to the B3 references.** 6NC5 ZN302 is four-coordinate (HOH485 1.90, His163 NE2 2.09, HOH493 2.14, His87 NE2 2.22; Asp91 **4.20/4.22 Å away**), against H-R-H rather than HHH; L1 7UHI/7UHJ put an intact β-lactam carbonyl at 2.53/2.63 Å on Zn1 with the ring N and C-carboxylate on Zn2; CSR-1 is 39.0% identical to L1 over a 221-residue core at 1.05 Å. *Figure:* the reference-value table (L1 2.63 / thermolysin 2.41 / CPA 2.98) and the similarity table. *Rests on:* `refstruct.py` measurements with PDB IDs and commands recorded. Honest and useful as a framing section. It is not a result about catalysis.

### Reportable with heavy caveats, not as findings

- **Arg229, not Lys226, is the substrate's carboxylate anchor in every structure we have.** 64.2% of 5000 frames at 3.65 Å mean; **3.58–3.66 Å across all 45 grid points while the substrate's other end swings 2.6 Å**. That rigidity is striking. *But:* measured with the substrate held by a 50 kcal/mol/Å² tether, in a pose 5.15 Å from the experimental one, in a trajectory whose zinc is six-coordinate. It is a hypothesis to re-test on a rebuilt complex, not a result.
- **Gln93 does not participate.** 0% occupancy in 10,000 MM frames, never within 7.86 Å of the carbonyl; ≥9.73 Å across all 45 grid points while the carbonyl moves 2.6 Å; charge-deletion contribution **−0.87 kcal/mol** against Arg229's +44.34. Two independent methods agreeing on a negative is the most robust claim in the project after the structural ones — and even this is pose-dependent, and the geometry was optimised with Gln93 in QM and scored with it in MM.
- **Asp92 binds Zn1 at every level of theory the project has run, and the crystal says it should not.** MM at 100 ns unrestrained (¶134/¶177), xTB QM/MM unrestrained at 25 ps (¶51) and 3 ps (¶157), DFT 1.6× steeper than xTB on the displacement coordinate (¶58/¶61). ¶151's MM population analysis then shows on/off are near-equal in free energy (basins 1.5–1.9 kcal/mol apart, populations within 0.2 kcal/mol), which correctly withdraws the "the orphaned carboxylate fills the vacancy" claim. **This is a real and interesting discrepancy between model and structure.** It is not yet a result, because (i) neither scan arm reaches the crystal's 4.20 Å, (ii) the free energy is MM with the other four donors restrained, (iii) the only route that reproduces the crystal sphere requires a protonation state the chemistry rules out, and (iv) the whole thing is measured in a complex with the wrong ligand pose.

### Publishable about the method / pipeline (this is where the real output is)

A methods or software note is genuinely available, and I would encourage it as a separate paper rather than diluting the enzyme paper:
- **Restraint laundering** (¶68/¶71): a chosen harmonic restraint in one run becomes an auto-detected holonomic constraint in the next, and nothing downstream can distinguish an imposed distance from a crystallographic one. With the implemented refusal and the chosen/detected split.
- **Rigid-water optimizer constraints becoming convergence criteria** (¶137–¶142): `$freeze` vs `$set`, why it bites only on structures written by a `rigidWater=True` MM run (0.9574 vs ~0.978 Å), and the 0 of 9 → 9 of 9 before/after.
- **Detection granularity** (¶175/¶176): a per-residue coordination restraint on a bidentate carboxylate restrains a distance, not a geometry, and the second oxygen rotates in. With ¶120 and ¶130 as the two worked failures.
- **Three upstream bugs**, with reproductions: ASH's `gxTBTheory` accepting `MMcharges`/`PC` and referencing neither (silent gas-phase QM/MM energies), the library-mode PC gradient, and `xtb.h` never declaring `xtb_getPCGradient`.
- **The silent-success class** (`confirm_produced`): job 31186038 sits in the inventory as COMPLETED having computed nothing.
- **Measured cost figures** that the field lacks: 750–760 ns/day for a 49k-atom system on an H100 (twice), 2.57 ps/h for GFN2-xTB QM/MM MD on 8 cores, ~26 min per ωB97X-D3(BJ)/def2-SVP/MM single point on 119 QM atoms at 37% efficiency on 64 cores, the MIG-slice vs full-card correction.

## PART 4 — WHAT A STRONG CSR-1 MECHANISM PAPER STILL NEEDS

Prioritised. Items 1–3 are prerequisites for everything else; nothing below item 3 is worth a core-hour until they land.

**1. A Michaelis complex whose ligand pose is anchored to experiment.** *Answers:* where meropenem actually sits — which every geometric, energetic and electrostatic number in the project is conditional on. *Why the paper fails without it:* the current pose is 5.15 Å (centroid) from the only measured B3 Michaelis complex, no docked pose puts an atom within 5 Å of the zinc, and the project's own acceptance gate has rejected it in every run. A referee who reads ¶156 would stop there. *Route (¶155/¶158, already correct):* transfer L1 7UHI/7UHJ's intact moxalactam and/or SMB-1 5AXO's hydrolysed meropenem into the CSR-1 frame on the Zn1 site, graft meropenem onto the shared bicyclic core, clash-check, and run the redocking control the IMP-1 precedent implies (re-place each template's own ligand by the same procedure and report RMSD). *Cost:* days of human time, no compute. *Depends on:* nothing. **Prerequisite for 2–11.**

**2. Both endpoints on one topology, built by reversing the hydrolysis.** *Answers:* whether the reaction coordinate between reactant and product is chemistry or a translation. *Why:* `neb` refuses endpoints differing in atom order, and a reactant prepared through its own `preprocess` will never match a template-derived product. ¶158 has this exactly right: "if the product comes from a template and the reactant stays where it is, the reaction coordinate between them is mostly a translation ... That failure would look entirely normal in the output." *Route:* build the product from 5AXO, then re-form C4x–N1x by `scan` through the product's own MM topology; 7UHI becomes the *check* (does the carbonyl land near 2.5–2.9 Å?) rather than the source. *Cost:* scan ~16 points × ~8.5 min = ~2–3 h on 8 cores, plus the build. *Depends on:* 1.

**3. An equilibration protocol that does not destroy the site it was built to preserve — and the reactant arm re-based on it.** *Answers:* whether a four-coordinate zinc with Asp92 in the second shell survives equilibration at all. *Why:* ¶177 — every reactant-side QM/MM result descends from a trajectory that is six-coordinate in 4995/5000 frames. *Route:* the `prod100c` treatment applied to the reactant: detection at 3.0 Å per **atom**, every donor it finds held at its crystal distance (both Asp92 oxygens), `coordination_redetect: true`, then **read the occupancy over the whole trajectory with `postprocess`, not the two-frame `md` report** — the two-frame report is precisely why this went unseen. *Cost:* 100 ns ≈ 4 h GPU per arm. *Depends on:* 1 and 2 if the pose changes (it will). **Prerequisite for 4–11.**

**4. The nucleophile's identity, origin and protonation state, settled by a method that can settle it.** *Answers:* the single question a referee in this field will ask first about a mono-zinc MBL. *Why:* P6 is still open at entry 177 and is marked "blocks all scans". ¶48 (−5.2 kcal/mol, constrained, def2-SVP) and ¶107 (pKa bookkeeping, +6–7 the other way) disagree **in sign**, and GFN2's error on protonated amines (5.3–7.7 kcal/mol) spans the disagreement. *What a referee expects:* for a Zn1-site mono-zinc enzyme, either a metal-bound hydroxide with its pKa justified (computed or by analogy with a measured system), or a second-shell water with a named general base and a demonstrated H-bond geometry — plus an explanation of how the hydroxide forms, since there is no Zn2 to bridge it. They will **not** accept a single constrained ΔE at def2-SVP, and they will not accept a resting state chosen by which trajectory happened to be run. *Route:* on the rebuilt complex, the VIM-2/Imhof criterion the project already adopted — equilibrate each state and keep the one that holds the crystal's site (¶113/¶114) — plus DFT single points at a larger basis on the relaxed, unconstrained geometries. *Cost:* 2 × (100 ns MM + ≥50 ps QM/MM) ≈ 2 × 24 h, plus 2–4 DFT single points at ~30 min each. *Depends on:* 1–3.

**5. Protonation and tautomer due diligence.** *Answers:* His88/His164/His53/His112/His150 tautomers and Asp92's charge. *Why:* ¶7 prescribed PROPKA/H++ in the very first week and it was never run; the tautomers came from a crystal file whose own hydrogens put His164 HE2 **0.76 Å from the zinc**; His164 NE2 is 3.2 Å from the developing oxyanion, so HID vs HIE decides whether the oxyanion has an H-bond donor. A referee will ask, and the answer "we took them from a deposited model's refined hydrogens" is refuted by the project's own ¶21. *Cost:* PROPKA/H++ minutes; one HIE sensitivity QM/MM optimisation ~1 h; one ASH-protonated-Asp92 control (`site.py protonate` exists) ~15 min. *Depends on:* 3. **Cheapest high-value item on the list.**

**6. The two elementary steps: scan → NEB → TS → frequencies → IRC, on ≥3 snapshots.** *Answers:* the barriers — the paper's actual content. *Why:* there is currently no path of any kind. *What a referee will not accept:* a barrier from a NEB whose reactant endpoint has a max gradient of 1.9e-01 (¶106); one saddle without exactly one imaginary frequency *and* that mode being the reaction coordinate (`check_ts_mode` exists — use it, and note its 0.3 floor is admittedly invented); a quasi-IRC presented as an IRC (¶26 is explicit that the default is ±0.15 Å displacement plus minimisation, "not a true mass-weighted IRC path"); a single-snapshot barrier with no spread (¶17 planned mean ± spread over ≥5 snapshots; ¶47 dropped it — reinstate at least 3). *Cost per step per snapshot:* scan ~2–3 h; NEB 12 images, nebts, roughly 1–2 days on 8 cores; partial Hessian over 15–25 atoms = 90–150 gradients at 60–70 s ≈ 2–3 h; IRC comparable. Two steps × 3 snapshots ≈ **2–3 weeks of wall time on a handful of 8-core jobs.** *Depends on:* 1–5.

**7. Level-of-theory validation, including the one Imhof actually did.** *Answers:* whether GFN2-xTB can be trusted for coordination number and for this chemistry. *Why:* P1 has been open since ¶9 and Imhof's own benchmark — the paper this project is modelled on — predicts semi-empirical errors of ~7.5 kcal/mol on 4→5 coordination and ~7 on bidentate→monodentate carboxylate, which are larger than anything the mechanism turns on. ¶58/¶65 are agreement on *conditional* questions evaluated on xTB geometries, which ¶59 and ¶64 both say is not the test. *Route:* a cluster model (Zn, both imidazoles, Asp92 acetate, both waters, truncated substrate ≈ 50–55 atoms, as ¶54 scoped) at GFN2 vs DFT, for coordination number, the Asp92 displacement, and the attack step; plus ωB97X-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVP(D) single points on every stationary point of the final paths. *Cost:* cluster benchmark is a day or two of small jobs; path single points ~26 min each × ~15–30 points × 2 steps ≈ **13–26 h on 24–32 cores**. *Depends on:* 6 for the path single points; the cluster benchmark depends on nothing and **should be started now**, in parallel with 1–3.

**8. Protonation of the leaving nitrogen.** *Answers:* what protonates N1x, and when. *Why:* with no Zn2 there is nothing to stabilise the anionic nitrogen, which is the paper's own central structural claim — so this is the step a referee will scrutinise hardest. The log already has the literature right: three papers converge on a water arriving *after* ring opening, with the anionic intermediate long-lived and its decay rate-determining (Lisa 2017, Das & Nair 2017), and ¶107 correctly reads "nothing is positioned to protonate N1x in the Michaelis complex" as the *expected* result rather than a defect. Lys226 NZ is 4.71–6.92 Å from N1x across 50 frames and cannot be the donor. *Route:* step 3 of ¶17 as its own scan/NEB, with the proton source treated as a hypothesis set (¶18's framing is sound); measure solvent access to N1x on the rebuilt trajectory. *Depends on:* 6.

**9. Per-residue roles — and a method that can actually reach R, Q and K.** *Answers:* the paper's title question. *Why it is currently impossible:* Arg90 and Lys226 are inside the QM region in all eleven systems, so charge deletion refuses them; only Arg229 and Gln93 are measurable, and mixing deletion with a parallel all-MM model in one table is not legitimate (¶153). *Route:* decide this before item 3's equilibration, because it is a model decision: either build the production system with Arg90, Lys226, Arg229 and Gln93 **all in MM** and decompose the whole set consistently, or do the roles by computational mutagenesis (Arg90→Ala/His, Lys226→Ala/His, and the 6DR8-like triple mutant as a di-zinc control). And in either case the deletions must be run **at both ends of the reaction coordinate** — an absolute interaction at one geometry (Arg229's +44.34) is not a catalytic contribution and must not be quoted as one. *Cost:* 2 single points per residue per state (~3 h each at 16 cores); mutants cost a full system each. *Depends on:* 3 (as a design input) and 6 (for the two-state differencing).

**10. The comparison with di-zinc enzymes, computed rather than cited.** *Answers:* what the absent Zn2 costs, which is the only way "the RQK triad substitutes for Zn2" becomes a result rather than a hypothesis. *Why:* the paper's thesis is comparative. A referee will ask for the di-zinc counterpart at the same level of theory, and will note that **every mono-zinc MBL in the literature carries its metal in the Zn2 site** (GOB-18, Sfh-I, B2 CphA) while CSR-1's is in Zn1 — which ¶124 identifies as the reason the CphA route failed twice and which is itself a genuine novelty worth foregrounding. *Route:* the same two steps on L1 (or on CSR-1's own 6DR8-equivalent triple mutant, which has a real crystal structure at 1.48 Å) at identical level, QM region and protocol. *Cost:* a second full campaign — effectively doubles the project. *Depends on:* 6. **This is the item most likely to be demanded and most likely to be refused on cost; decide early whether to do it or to scope the paper so it is not needed.**

**11. The Asp92 free energy, if the discrepancy is still live after 1–3.** *Answers:* whether Asp92 on/off the metal is an equilibrium, which the kinetics argument (¶133) makes mechanistically interesting. *Why it is not urgent:* ¶157's correction makes it affordable (26 windows × 40.9 h ≈ 8500 core-hours, ~2 days wall, every window inside the 72 h limit), but ¶157 is also right that "a PMF on the Asp92 coordinate computed from that pose would be an accurate free energy for the wrong complex". *Depends on:* 1–3. Run it only if the rebuilt complex still shows the discrepancy.

### Experimental and literature comparison — and this is a structural problem for the paper

**There is no published kinetics for CSR-1 with meropenem and no published mechanistic study** (¶22, ¶40: "no published mechanistic study or meropenem kinetics for CSR-1"; the only CSR-1 paper, Pedroso 2020, is characterisation with no computational methods to replicate). So a computed barrier has **nothing to be validated against**. A referee will ask what the number means. Options, in order of strength:

1. **Get kinetics measured** — kcat/Km for meropenem, the pH-rate profile (which would bear directly on Lys226's protonation; Imhof made exactly this argument from EcoRV's pH 8.5 optimum, and ¶114 notes "the pH-optimum argument is one we could make too"), and the metal stoichiometry/ITC for the mono-zinc claim (the ITC numbers in ¶22, α ≈ 2 µM / β ≈ 150 µM, are second-hand from a literature summary and should be verified or re-measured). A collaborator is worth more here than any amount of compute.
2. **Anchor to a measured homologue** at identical level of theory — item 10.
3. **At minimum, state the limitation explicitly** and compare the computed barrier to the *class* (CphA's PMF ≈ 14 kcal/mol, the SMB-1 and NDM-1 QM/MM barriers) while being clear that the enzyme's own turnover is unmeasured.

Two further literature items the paper needs to face rather than bury: the **N-terminal truncation** question (CSR-1 is reported active only after truncation; the model is non-truncated with residues 1–14 modelled in from no experimental coordinates, sitting 9–13 Å from the zinc — ¶40 overstated this, ¶41 correctly narrowed it, and it is still unresolved and a referee will ask), and the fact that **every reference Michaelis complex is di-zinc** so the C3-carboxylate contact in each template is to a metal CSR-1 does not have (¶156/¶158 both flag this correctly — it is the thing the transfer is for, not a reason to avoid it).

## One closing judgement

The record is unusually honest — it catches its own errors, retracts claims in place, and the corrections are nearly always in the right direction. ¶59 deflating ¶58, ¶64 deflating ¶63, ¶127 catching the gate that assumed the hypothesis, ¶141/¶142 refusing to let a sweeping claim stand, ¶151 withdrawing "the vacancy is filled" on the user's challenge, ¶165 and ¶171 both admitting a design decision was inferred from config files instead of read from the log, ¶176 admitting detection was never per-atom, ¶177 overturning ¶134/¶165/¶174 the same day: this is good practice and rare.

But the honesty has mostly been spent auditing the pipeline. Three months in, the project knows a great deal about how mcube can go wrong and almost nothing about how CSR-1 works. The two findings that matter most — that the ligand pose is 5.15 Å from the only experimental reference, and that every equilibration produces a six-coordinate zinc against the crystal's four — were both measured in the last four days, and both invalidate most of what came before them. That is the right place to be *if* the next move is the rebuild (items 1–3) and not another diagnostic. The failure mode to watch for is the one ¶131 named about the log itself: a fix that was prescribed and not carried out is indistinguishable, later, from a fix that landed. ¶120 prescribed the coordination fix on 28 October; ¶177 established on 2 November that it never worked.
